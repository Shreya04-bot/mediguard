== SCREENING AND DIAGNOSIS ==

Screening: All individuals aged 30 years and above should be screened for type 2
diabetes. Screening should start earlier for people with any of: family history of
diabetes; overweight/obesity (BMI >=23 kg/m2) or increased waist circumference
(>90 cm men, >80 cm women); history of hypertension (>=130/80 mmHg) or on
antihypertensive treatment; dyslipidaemia; sedentary lifestyle; prior gestational
diabetes or a baby born >3.5 kg; history of cardiovascular disease; or PCOS with
acanthosis nigricans. If glucose tolerance is normal, retest in 3 years; retest annually
if prediabetic.

Diagnostic criteria (any one, confirmed by a repeat test): fasting plasma glucose
>=126 mg/dL; 2-hour post-75g-OGTT glucose >=200 mg/dL; HbA1c >=6.5%; or classic
symptoms plus a random plasma glucose >=200 mg/dL. Prediabetes is defined as
impaired fasting glucose (100-125 mg/dL per ADA, or 110-125 per WHO) or impaired
glucose tolerance (140-199 mg/dL 2-hour OGTT), or HbA1c 5.7-6.4%.

== TARGETS FOR METABOLIC CONTROL ==

Ideal targets: fasting plasma glucose 80-110 mg/dL; 2-hour postprandial glucose
120-140 mg/dL; blood pressure <130/80 mmHg; BMI 20-23 kg/m2; HbA1c <7%.
Lipid targets: total cholesterol <200 mg/dL; HDL >40 mg/dL (men) / >50 mg/dL
(women); LDL <100 mg/dL (or <70 mg/dL for those at high cardiovascular risk —
established CVD, smoking, obesity, hypertension, family history of premature CVD);
non-HDL cholesterol <130 mg/dL; triglycerides <150 mg/dL. Targets should be
individualized — tighter in younger patients without complications, relaxed with
advancing age or comorbidity.

== NON-PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT ==

Medical nutrition therapy is individualized around family eating patterns: roughly
55-60% of energy from complex, high-fibre carbohydrates; 12-15% from protein;
20-30% from fat (saturated fat <=10% of energy, trans fat <1%, preference for
MUFA-rich oils like mustard/rice bran/groundnut). Added salt <5 g/day generally,
<3 g/day if hypertensive. Alcohol best avoided. Recommended physical activity: about
150 minutes/week of aerobic activity plus resistance training at least twice weekly
and flexibility work. Tobacco use is to be stopped completely. Yoga is noted as a
traditional adjunct with emerging evidence for benefit on glycaemic control and
stress, though more high-quality studies are called for.

== PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT ==

Metformin is first-line therapy for most patients (start 500 mg after meals, titrate by
500 mg every 2 weeks toward a maximum of 2500 mg/day), contraindicated in renal
insufficiency (dose-adjust below eGFR 60, halve below 45, stop below 30 mL/min),
hepatic/respiratory insufficiency, recent MI, heart failure, or ketoacidosis. If HbA1c
remains above target on metformin monotherapy, second-line options include
sulfonylureas (effective, inexpensive, but hypoglycaemia/weight-gain risk),
DPP-4 inhibitors (low hypoglycaemia risk, weight-neutral, costlier), SGLT2 inhibitors
(weight/BP-lowering, genitourinary infection risk), thiazolidinediones, alpha-
glucosidase inhibitors, glinides, or GLP-1 receptor agonists. Insulin should be started
without delay whenever indicated — significant symptomatic hyperglycaemia, fasting
glucose >270 mg/dL or HbA1c >9% at diagnosis, ketosis, severe infection, or failure of
maximal oral combination therapy — rather than treated as a last resort.

== COMPLICATIONS ==

Diabetic retinopathy: dilated retinal exam at diagnosis (type 2) or within 5 years
(type 1), then annually; ~90% of diabetes-related blindness is preventable with early
detection and treatment. Diabetic nephropathy: annual urine albumin-creatinine ratio
and serum creatinine/eGFR from diagnosis; ACE inhibitors or ARBs (never combined)
are first-line for hypertension with nephropathy; refer to nephrology for resistant
hypertension, serum potassium >5.5 meq/L, nephrotic-range proteinuria, or eGFR
<60 mL/min. Diabetic neuropathy and diabetic foot: annual comprehensive foot exam
(10g monofilament, 128Hz tuning fork, vascular pulses), with risk stratified into
low/moderate/high/active-disease categories that set follow-up frequency from
annual up to weekly. Foot ulceration should be referred to a multidisciplinary foot
service within 24 hours.

== COMORBID CONDITIONS ==

Hypertension: blood pressure should be checked at every visit; >140/90 mmHg is
the threshold to initiate treatment (recheck after 1 week to confirm). First-line
monotherapy is an ACE inhibitor or ARB (never combined), calcium channel blocker,
or (third-line) a selective beta blocker/diuretic. If BP remains >140/90 after a month
of monotherapy, add a second agent from a different class; if still uncontrolled on two
optimally-dosed agents, refer to a specialist. Severe hypertension at presentation
(>180/110 mmHg, or evidence of end-organ damage) warrants starting two drugs
immediately and urgent specialist referral. People with both diabetes and
hypertension should be managed more aggressively than hypertension alone, given
the compounded stroke/MI/nephropathy risk.

Dyslipidaemia: screen all patients with diabetes; treat aggressively per the lipid
targets above. Obesity: lifestyle modification is the mainstay; bariatric surgery may
be considered in morbid obesity. Tuberculosis: India has a high burden of TB-diabetes
comorbidity (~25% of TB patients found to have diabetes); a chest X-ray is advised at
initial evaluation where clinically indicated, and insulin is preferred over oral agents
when diabetes is uncontrolled in the presence of active TB.

== DIABETES AND PREGNANCY ==

Universal screening of all pregnant women for gestational diabetes is preferred over
selective (risk-factor-based) screening. A 75g OGTT is done at 24-28 weeks using
IADPSG cut-offs (fasting >=92, 1-hour >=180, 2-hour >=153 mg/dL — any one value
diagnostic), or the non-fasting DIPSI single-value test (2-hour >=140 mg/dL) where
fasting testing isn't practical. Lifestyle/nutrition therapy is first-line; most patients
progressing beyond that need insulin (oral agents other than metformin are generally
avoided in pregnancy). All women with gestational diabetes need a postpartum OGTT
at 6-8 weeks, since 50-70% go on to develop diabetes within 5-10 years.
