NOTE ON THIS DOCUMENT: this is a narrower, more limited summary than the
ICMR diabetes and WHO hypertension documents in this corpus — it is
composed from the published abstract, methods, and findings of the 2019
risk-chart paper plus the publicly described scope of the HEARTS package,
not from the full text of either document (which could not be retrieved
in this session). It covers the risk-stratification *approach*, not a
complete clinical management protocol. For full clinical detail, consult
the sources above directly.

== WHY RISK-BASED, NOT SINGLE-FACTOR, ASSESSMENT ==

Both sources describe a shift away from treating individual risk factors
(blood pressure, cholesterol) in isolation, toward estimating a person's
total absolute 10-year cardiovascular disease risk from their combined
risk-factor profile, then using that total-risk estimate to guide
treatment intensity. The HEARTS package frames this explicitly: CVD
develops from the combination of tobacco use, unhealthy diet, physical
inactivity, obesity, elevated blood pressure, dyslipidaemia, and elevated
blood glucose acting together — total risk depends on the whole profile,
not any single factor crossing a threshold in isolation.

== THE WHO CVD RISK CHARTS (2019 REVISION) ==

The revised WHO risk prediction models estimate a person's 10-year risk
of a fatal or non-fatal cardiovascular event (myocardial infarction or
stroke) from five inputs: age, sex, smoking status, systolic blood
pressure, and either total cholesterol (a "laboratory-based" chart) or
BMI as a substitute where cholesterol testing isn't available (a
"non-laboratory-based" chart) — plus history of diabetes. This
lab-free option is deliberately included so risk estimation doesn't have
to wait on lab infrastructure that may not be available in every
primary-care setting.

The models were derived from 376,177 individuals across 85 cohorts
(19,333 incident cardiovascular events over 10 years of follow-up) and
externally validated in a further 19 cohorts (1,096,061 individuals,
25,950 events), with good discrimination (Harrell's C-index 0.685-0.833
across validation cohorts). Because absolute risk for an identical risk
factor profile varies substantially by region — the paper's own
worked example: a 60-year-old male smoker, non-diabetic, systolic BP
140 mmHg, total cholesterol 5 mmol/L, has an estimated 10-year risk
ranging from 11% (Andean Latin America) to 30% (Central Asia) — the
charts are calibrated separately for 21 global regions rather than using
one universal chart.

== HOW RISK CATEGORIES ARE USED CLINICALLY ==

The general pattern across WHO/ISH-style risk charts (this project's
region-specific banding may vary; consult the official chart for the
applicable region) is to bucket predicted 10-year risk into bands — low,
moderate, high, and very high — with treatment intensity escalating by
band: lifestyle counselling alone at the low end, and progressively
stronger cases for statin and/or antihypertensive pharmacotherapy as
predicted risk rises, independent of whether any single input (e.g. blood
pressure) has individually crossed a diagnostic threshold. This is the
key practical implication for a decision-support tool: two people with
identical blood pressure can warrant different management if their
overall risk profiles (age, smoking, cholesterol, diabetes status)
differ.

== INTEGRATION WITH COMORBIDITIES ==

Both this corpus's ICMR diabetes document and WHO hypertension document
describe risk-based escalation for their respective conditions
(cardiovascular risk stratifying LDL targets in the diabetes guideline;
elevated baseline CVD risk lowering the blood-pressure treatment
threshold in the hypertension guideline). The risk-chart approach
described here is the common thread connecting them: diabetes,
hypertension, and dyslipidaemia are evaluated individually by their own
guidelines, but a person's overall cardiovascular risk is what should
ultimately guide how aggressively each is treated.
